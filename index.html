      Basics

      The shot, the milk, the foam, and the syrups everything else is built on.

      Dial in the shot

      Start the timer when you start the shot. Stop it when the scale reads 36 g.

      (Dialing in)

      1. Weigh 18 g of ground coffee into the basket, level it and tamp evenly.
      2. Put the cup on a scale. Start the shot and the timer together.
      3. Stop at 36 g and check the time.
      4. Under 25 seconds and sour or thin: grind finer. Over 30 seconds and bitter or harsh: grind coarser.
      5. For a single, use 9 g in the single basket and stop at 18 g, in the same 25–30 seconds. Keep the grinder on the double setting and fix a fast single by adding coffee, up to 10 g.
      6. Taste straight shots while dialing in, not lattes. Milk and sugar hide problems.
      7. Re-check every morning, and whenever you open a new bag of beans.

      (Sweet, milky drinks)

      • Try a shorter shot for sweet drinks: 18 g in, 30 g out. A more concentrated shot cuts through milk and sugar.
      • Pour hot espresso onto the sweetener first and stir before adding ice or milk. Sugar, condensed milk and chocolate dissolve badly in cold liquid.
      • Use bigger ice cubes, and fewer of them. Melting ice is the main reason iced lattes taste thin. Treat 100 g as the maximum.
      • Steam milk to 55–65 °C.

      (Cold foam rules)

      • Cream must outweigh milk. More cream gives a thick foam that floats; more milk gives a thin foam that sinks.
      • Don't skip the milk entirely. A little milk stops the cream turning into stiff whipped cream.
      • Keep cream fridge-cold. Froth until thick but still pourable, then pour slowly on top.
      • All-purpose cream works in almost every foam here.
